On hearing from Marty that the hotel room's bathing facilities included only _a mere shower,_ the tiger declared that they were not up to the requirements of a deity performing a holistic cleansing ritual, and offered to take Marty to his own baths.  The sable was initially hesitant - they were comfy, unclothed, and in private - but gave in to his striped benefactor's amused reassurances that clothing would not be necessary for the trip.  "Nobody will even know we've left your room," he reassured.  "Close your eyes and hold on to me, though - this _is_ going to feel a bit strange, and you may not want to see it."

Marty closed his eyes, and felt the tiger shield his face with one paw, as well.  Then, suddenly, gravity went haywire.  Marty felt himself go into free-fall, held in place only by the tiger's arms, and stifled a yelp.  It was brief; as his sense of "down" returned, he felt it swing wildly.  As things stabilized, it seemed that down had changed from _against_ the tiger's chest to _along_ the tiger's chest, as though the big cat were suddenly standing.  As he tried to slow his heart back down, in the background, he could hear that the low hum and gentle whoosh of his hotel room's ventilation had been replaced by the gentle chirping of what sounded like crickets.  The air was warmer, and more humid.  "Wh...whoa.  Is...is it safe to open my eyes, yet?"

The tiger's voice was ripe with amusement.  "Slowly, but yes, you can open your eyes, pet."

Marty peeked out of the eye closest to his tiger's chest, from which he could see that the protective paw still partially shielded his view.  The big cat, face lit by a dim glow from somewhere nearby, was smiling down at him, and seemed to be watching his expression as Marty started to really see.  There was some kind of dappled, textured light coming from above and behind the tiger, which Marty thought looked sort of like stars in a moonless night sky.  He slowly opened his other eye.  The stars were definitely not the only light, or even most of the light.  Gentle, warm luminance seemed to come from all around.  As the tiger slowly removed his paw, Marty gasped.

Most of the light seemed to come from the trees.  They were in a forest, or possibly a garden; the distinction here made little difference.  Blooms with beautiful, luminous, feathery stigma and filaments extending from among their colorful petals, themselves veined with contrasting luminescence, provided most of the light immediately around them.  Closer to the ground, mushrooms and fungi of various shapes and sizes emitted a pleasant glow from beneath their caps, or highlighted trees or stones with emanations directly from their stems or shelves.  Clusters of leaves overhead reflected warm light down toward them, though its source was not readily apparent.  And just ahead of the tiger, in the direction he appeared to be gradually heading, there were gently glowing fish and mollusks moving slowly around a large pool of water, itself ringed with more of the bioluminescent fungi and wreathed in slowly rising steam.  The illuminated leaves in the canopy above filtered the starlight, letting through patches of the brightest view of the milky way that Marty had ever seen.

The sable's eyes were as wide as the tiger's were amused.  "What is this place?" he asked in an awed whisper.

"This," the tiger intoned, "is a _proper_ bath."  There was a quiet gurgling sound from the water's edge as he stepped smoothly in.  "And you, my pet, are about to be _properly_ bathed."

"Is...is it safe?" Marty asked.

"No, I'm afraid not," came the reply.  "I'm told there's a _very large tiger_ in it.  Think it's worth the risk?"  The tiger's grin was the biggest he'd ever seen on any cat.  "I hear they can _bite,"_ he said, punctuating the last word with a snap of his jaws and a chuckle at Marty.

Marty leaned a little harder against the big cat's chest.  "I think I'll take my chances, in _that_ case."

The quiet gurgle of the inverse tiger's black-furred form passing through the water grew gradually louder as he went gradually deeper, and quieted again as he slowed.  "Alright, here you go," he said, as he reached the point in his progression at which he slowly began to dip Marty into the water, as well.

"Ooh.  _Oh wow,_ it's really hot," said the sable.

"Yes.  _Proper._  We'll go slow.  I promise, it won't hurt you," he said, "not unless you find relaxation damaging."

Marty nodded, and tried to steel himself for the transition.  "So is this just...a spot you knew of?" the sable asked.

"Made, sort of," the tiger answered, still holding Marty as he slowly lowered both of them into the water.  "The spring needed a bit of encouragement, the stones some massage.  The creatures in the water seemed happiest here, and seem to like what I bring them, as well as what falls in.  I simply brought them together, and attend to their harmony."

Marty tried to ignore the sensation of the hot springwater lapping over his waist.  "So this is yours?"

"In the sense that I tend to it, yes, I suppose it is."

"How long have you been tending to it?" Marty asked.

The tiger grinned.  "A very, _very_ long time.  We have a rich history together."  He paused to pull Marty slowly away from him, still offering some support, but gently encouraging the smaller Marty to float on his own.  "There, relax - there you go.  See?  You can float comfortably here - just a little farther."

"Do you live here?"  Marty asked, still trying to relax.  The tiger still had a hand under his back, supporting him right in the hollow between his shoulder blades, but it was their last point of contact.

"As nearly as I live anywhere.  I return here when I want somewhere to be myself, and if I find things or creatures that delight me, and I think they might enjoy it too, sometimes, I bring them here."  He let go of Marty completely for a moment, watching the sable struggle a little to relax into the water.  Gently, he placed a fingertip on Marty's forehead.  "Shhh, pet.  Deep breaths.  Let the water carry you, and just relax."  

Marty let himself focus on the gentle contact with his forehead, slowly closed his eyes, and let his body rest in the gently rippling water.  He took a deep breath, let it out slowly.  Took another.  Without opening his eyes, asked, "what kinds of creatures?"

Marty heard as well as felt a quiet purr start from the big cat's chest.  "Well, most recently," the tiger said, and paused, lifting his finger from Marty's forehead and arranging his own body, _"...you."_  At this last, Marty felt a slow and gentle pressure well up underneath him as the tiger's form floated slowly up into contact with his own, their fur mediating the contact.  The sable eventually felt himself lifted slightly out of the water by his protector's buoyancy, his arms resting on the tiger's arms, his head resting in the hollow between his collar bones.  The tiger took a deep breath and let it out slowly, and Marty felt his entire body move with the big cat's respiration.

Marty felt, for a moment, like he needed to find something else to say, or he risked letting the conversation fall.  But as his tiger took another deep breath beneath him, he realized the moment didn't really need any more words.  The two of them were laying, relaxed, together, in the spring, and the moment felt complete in itself; so he released the search for words, and felt it depart from him as though it were sinking into the hot spring beneath the two of them.  Instead, he tried to see if he could make his breathing complement the tiger's, and as their bodies began to move in time with one another, he felt the gentle rumble of the tiger's low, pleased purr swell louder in his own chest.  The cadence was not easy to keep - the tiger's breaths were long and deep, and got longer and deeper the further he relaxed - but the closer he got, the easier it became.  The slow, deep breaths encouraged his own body to relax, and the tiger's purr reverberating through both of them gave the sable such a sense of connection with the god beneath him that with his eyes still closed, he began having trouble feeling where his own body ended.

After a couple of minutes, through the pleasant haze that had enveloped his mind, Marty distantly felt his master stir ever so slightly.  His own left arm (he had to double-check to be sure) slipped slightly lower in the water; the tiger's arm had moved gently out from under it.  He felt a weight on his chest as the big cat placed that paw on Marty's chest and gently pet him with it.  "Sshhh, remain at peace, little one," he heard, in a voice so deep that it was not so much speech as it was modulated purring, still resonating through him.  "I love that you breathe with me.  The harmony, the trust, feels lovely."  Another slow, deep breath.  "We will return to that in a moment, once I explain what is coming.  To fully heal you, you must walk through the memory with me one more time _without remembering_ walking through it, or the act of recall _itself_ will leave echoes, and make a full cleanse impossible."

"I don't think I know how to stop remembering," Marty said.  

"No one in the world knows how to _consciously_ stop themselves; that is nearly impossible," the tiger explained.  "I will take care of that for you."  Instantly, he felt Marty's anxiety rise, just as he'd been expecting.

"W...will I feel it?  Will I still be me?  Will I be okay?"  Marty could feel his breathing speed up, but so too could he feel the intensity of the tiger's calming purrs and his petting rising to match.

"Sshh, you will be just fine, little one.  I will be right here with you.  You will be fully yourself the whole time.  In the moment, is feels no different; you simply won't remember that moment later on.  When you fully remember again, you will feel as if you had dozed off, and just come awake again.  It will feel familiar; your body does this for you _normally_ when you doze off."  The tiger punctuated the ensuing silence with more quiet purring and calming pets, giving the sable time to process and calm his nerves.

"If you _gave_ me the memory so easily before in my room, why does it take so much more work to remove it?"

"Remember I said it would begin to leave scars?  When you relived that memory for the first time, you were not _simply recalling_ the memory.  One of the ways that memories change over time, and don't always affect us the same way, is that you bring who you are at the moment to every experience you relive; and the relived experience is reflected back into you, as well.  Recalling painful memories can inflict fresh wounds.  To take back what I gave, I must also heal the damage it did within you."

"Will I know when you start?"

"You would not know it unless I told you, but it is better if I do not tell you, little one.  If you feel it, and it makes you uncomfortable, tell me, and we will stop.  Alright?"  He felt Marty's nod against his chest.  "Okay.  We will start in a little bit, but first, get comfortable again.  You were reaching a trance state, and it will be easiest - and most enjoyable - to begin and end there.  Breathe with me again, rest with me, and float.  I have you.  Nothing will hurt you.  _Be at peace, little one."_

Marty wanted to melt a little more every time the big cat called him "little one."  He took a few deep breaths on his own, trying to gradually slow their cadence and fall into sync with the tiger's slow, deep breaths again.  After a few breaths, he managed to match the tiger's cadence, and hoped to fall back into the relaxed headspace he had just temporarily left; but his mind wouldn't slow down.  _And why should it?_ asked a small voice from the back of his head.  _Here you are, about to have hot tub brain surgery to remove a rape, and you're supposed to be relaxed about it?_  His breathing faltered; he felt his body move disharmoniously against the purring god beneath him.  His anxiety spiked, and he felt serious worries start to amass.

The quiet was ruffled by a gentle stirring sound in the water as the tiger reached down and gently guided Marty's spread arms down toward his sides.  His big paws found the sable's, and wordlessly, he pressed those smaller palms against his own sides, as if to say, by pure body language, "hold onto me."  He felt the sable answer by working his short claws into the tiger's fur and holding on.  _Good,_ he thought.  He purred a little more, and brought his paws up to rest on either side of Marty's head.  He gently took the sable's head in both paws, just letting them rest there for a moment before slowly starting to roll it back and forth, gently back and forth, just a little bit; and then began to accompany each roll with a touch.  He set one finger down just above Marty's nose, and as he rolled his head the other direction, that finger traced a line up the sable's muzzle, between his eyes, and up over the top of his head; then trading for the same finger on the opposite hand, and tracing the same line again as he gently rolled the sable's head back the other direction.  Gentle strokes, gentle motions; and slowly, gradually, Marty calmed down.

To Marty, the effect was profound.  He wasn't used to anyone else caring so much about how he was able to regulate his own anxieties, never mind paying attention to it and _helping._  The invitation to hold on was unexpected, but welcome.  The slow, methodical, single-digit petting over the top of his head felt blissfull.  The tiger's slow, gentle handling of him was calming on an animal level, a visceral reminder that he was safe in these paws.  Eventually, somewhere between the bliss of those pets and _accidentally_ falling back into that deep, connected breathing cadence, his mind stopped racing, and he started to feel himself drifting.  After a minute, the tiger let his head go, and guided his arms back out to his sides, letting them rest atop his own arms again, and the two just drifted together in the calming hot spring.  

A minute later again, and the tiger spoke, still in the breathing cadence.  "It is lovely resting together like this, but little one, it is almost time."  Another slow breath.  "Remember, I'm right here with you.  I will not leave you, and nothing can take you from me."  Another deep breath.  "In a moment, I need you to walk through your memory with the coyote one last time.  Remember, you're safe.  He can't hurt you."  Another slow breath, this time accompanied by gentle pets on his chest, calming Marty almost before he felt his anxiety rise.  "This is the last time you ever need to remember it again.  Be brave for me.  I'll be right here with you the whole time."  Another deep breath.  "When you remember, if there are scars from the hurt, they will resonate in your mind."  Another slow breath.  "When they resonate, I can see them; when I see them, I will soothe them."  Another deep breath.  "Remember, I'll be right here with you.  You're _safe._  You'll be safe the whole time."  

There turned out to be a cadence to the recalling, also: when things got too overwhelming, the tiger used a shell to dip soothing water from the spring and gently let it pour over the sable, using this to calm and connect him to his body again.  Marty didn't realize it in the moment, but the tiger had been right: he was entirely unaware that he wasn't forming new memories during the process.  His mind was still moving forward, recounting the painful memories from the coyote, feeling the pain, the sorrow, the betrayal, connecting back with himself and with his protector when it became too much, and then moving on again when his resolve returned.  Less visible from Marty's forward-looking perspective was that as he forged ahead with recalling the memory, behind him, that memory and its effects slowly vanished, like smoke dissipating in still air.  

Eventually, Marty reached the end, where that fork of his memory in the alley abruptly ended.  He felt the flow of warm water over his body from the tiger's shell, let it calm his anxious shivers.  He let it pull him away from that traumatic end to where he _physically_ was, resting spread-eagle on a purring cat-god, as the last of the pain and trauma slowly faded.  He wanted to cry, and couldn't entirely understand why; but at the tiger's encouragement, he let the tears flow, down his cheeks, to the back of his head, down onto his protector.  He could almost have sworn he could feel them sinking into the pool below, taking the last of the hurt and fear with them as the tiger's unrelenting, comforting purr resonated through his body, and his petting in gentle strokes down Marty's body calmed and reassured him.  After a moment, he felt the big cat's fingers lace together over his chest, and then come to rest atop it; the weight of his arms pinned the sable to the big cat's chest.  The sensation felt secure, and reassuring.  He rested his own arms atop the tiger's, fingers laced the same way, and let out a huge sigh.

"Little one," the tiger said after a moment, "I must ask you a question."

"What is it?"

"That night, when you came out the back door into the alley - what was it you saw?"

Marty thought about this.  "I...I saw the coyote emerge from the door," he answered.  "I ducked down to try to hide, and then somehow, _you_ stepped around the dumpster instead of him."

The question seemed strange.  "Well...yeah, I think.  I know you saved me from something way worse that he had planned, but..."  Suddenly, it clicked.  Marty's breath caught in his throat, and he felt a huge grin forming on his face; but it did not outpace the tiger's laugh, mirthful and joyous, as Marty squirmed with excitement on his chest.

"You're free, little one!" he proclaimed.

Marty sighed in relief.  "Wow.  So now I _only_ need to remember you.  _Rrrff."_  He shivered theatrically, which made the tiger chuckle.  "So now what?"

"Well, I suppose at some point, we need to return to complete relaxation, so I can let you start remembering normally again."

Marty looked up at the tiger, who was already fairly blissfully relaxed, and got an excellent view of the underside of his chin.  "Yes, but _then_ what?"

The tiger chuckled again.  "You're mighty playful.  I like that, that'll come in handy when I offer you another bite at the apple later."

"...wait..."  There was a long pause.  "...you're not..."  Pause again.  "...do you mean that?  Do you _really_ mean that?!"

"Absolutely I do.  You seem enthusiastic?"

"You can't just _tell me things_ like this if I'm not gonna remember them later!"  Marty sighed in mock exasperation.  "I...I'm not gonna have any idea what to do, but _yes I'm enthusiastic,"_ the sable said.

"Well, rejoice for your future self, little one.  Now I'm _definitely_ looking forward to you figuring this out again in a few minutes."  Even without seeing the tiger's face, Marty could hear the grin in his voice.  

"Good luck, I guess, future me; _don't fuck this up._  And now I have to relax!"  He gestured expressively before re-lacing his fingers atop the tiger's.

After a moment, the big cat asked, "would you perhaps like some assistance?"

"...maybe?"

"Hold still."  The tiger lifted his paws from Marty's chest, and instead engulfed the sable's entire head in his paws.

There was a wave of mild euphoria, and Marty's inner monologue stopped in its tracks.  Speech left him.  Another wave washed over him, this one of placid, tranquil calm.  His mind was held in delicious, benevolent suspense, like being tied up and forced to watch a beautiful sunset.  His breathing slowed, and again fell into complementary cadence with the tiger's.  

"I'm sorry, little one," the tiger said to nobody in particular, since he knew there was no way Marty could hear him.  "With you, I do not like to be so heavy-handed, but you won't remember this, and we are _both_ going to love what's coming."  Gently, he lowered the sable's mind to float lightly on the sea of sleep, then re-laced his fingers over Marty's chest, and waited for him to come back around.

A few minutes passed.

Marty slowly became aware of a purr coming from beneath him, and realized he must've drifted off for a moment.  _You will feel as if you had dozed off, and just come awake again,_ he remembered.  He felt a weight across his chest; he looked down, and saw the tiger's paws resting laced together atop it.  "Did...did I _just_ drift off?  Has it happened yet?"

"It's already done.  I think the self you cannot remember would want you to know that you barely felt a thing."  He smirked, and hoped Marty could not hear it in his voice.  "You're free, little one!"

Marty sighed in relief, fuller this time than last, though he could not have remembered.  "So now I _only_ need to remember you?"

"So it would seem.  Tell me, do you recall what you saw when you came out the door into the alley?"

"I remember stumbling to the far side of the dumpster, falling into the loose trash bags, seeing the coyoute come out the door - but then somehow, it was you that came around the dumpster, not him," the sable replied.

"And is that the _only_ thing you remember happening right then?"

"Well, yeah, I..."  Marty's eyes snapped open in realization.  "Oh my god, it is!  Or at least, it is now!"  Relief came off the sable in almost palpable waves.  "Thank you thank you thank you - I would _so much rather_ just remember time with _you._  That was..." the sable shivered.  "I still can barely bellieve it even happened."  He put his own arms over the tiger's laced fingers where they rested across his chest, and hugged the big cat's paws tighter to himself; in answer, Marty felt the tiger's arms fully encircle him and hug tightly.  

"I'm proud of you, little one.  You were very brave," the tiger said, and felt Marty melt against him.  "I'm afraid, though, that water cat or not, there is only so long a hot spring is comfortable.  Shall we dry off?"  

Marty sighed and nodded, his ears drooping a bit.  "I don't want this moment to end, but I guess it has to some time, huh."

They floated to the far side of the spring.  The tiger rolled Marty off his chest near the edge of the pool, where Marty could reach the stones that ringed it; the sable propped his elbows on one particularly comfortable-looking rounded rock, and rested his head on his arms while he watched the tiger wade toward a set of stairs cut into the side of the pool.  "Let me dry off first, then I'll take care of you.  For now, enjoy the warmth for a little longer."  The tiger emerged slowly from the pool, wading out at a languid pace meant to let as much of the water streaming from his fur as possible run off into the pool as he rose from it.  

Marty stared.  Most of his time with the big cat had been spent at extremely close range; almost entirely in direct physical contact, even.  Now, with a little distance, he realized the tiger was larger than he'd thought.  _Seven feet was such a short guess,_ he thought.  _He's gotta be at least eight, maybe nine feet tall._  Even looking directly at him, it was difficult to see exactly how big he was.  The soft glow of the flowers, fungi and starlight, while there were a lot of them, didn't illuminate anything - or anyone - very brightly.  When the tiger moved, the fur over his hulking, mostly-black form constantly seemed to merge with the darkness between them, making it difficult to see just where his body ended.  Were it not for his slim, wandering golden stripes, Marty thought, it almost would've been hard to see him at all.  Eventually the whole enormous cat stepped out onto a flagstoned courtyard, where towels waited on a bench just out of sight behind some of the bushes.  

The tiger unfolded a towel and stepped back into view, and as he set to drying himself off, Marty found his mood starting to sag a bit.  This was the last thing they had set out to do, and it seemed to be ending.  After a moment or two, he managed to work up the nerve to ask about it.  "So...do I have to go back, now?"

"It would be wise to return at some point, but there's no particular hurry unless you _want_ to go back."  The tiger looked at Marty.  _"Do_ you want to go back?"

Marty stumbled over the answer.  "Er, well...I mean...no, not really, I...  No.  Actually, just no.  I'm happy I get to be with you again.  I'm not in a hurry either!"  The tiger nodded and went back to drying himself, and Marty silently congratulated himself on sounding like a blithering idiot.

"Oh, well!" said the tiger.  "That's good.  I'd hate to lose you so soon after we went to such trouble finding each other again.  And you know, little one, if you _did want_ to leave, I would take you back."  He winked at Marty.  "But I suspected you might not."

Marty smiled half-heartedly.  He was sure the tiger didn't feel the same way about this that he did, at least not now that things were somewhat sorted out.  "I'm still not totally sure I'm not dreaming.  I was pretty sure I would never see you again.  That I was crazy, that I'd been seeing things.  If this is a dream, though..."  Marty motioned as though trying to wave someone away.  "...just a few more minutes, Mom, I'm not ready to wake up yet."

Marty sighed, and rested his chin on his crossed arms.  "Am I going to be in the way if I stay with you a little longer?"

"No, little one.  I would not offer if you would be.  Besides, I have, ah...I believe the modern phrase is 'cleared my schedule' for a bit."

"Oh, what for?"  Marty asked.  

The tiger stopped, fixed Marty with a stare, and stopped moving entirely.  The sable could barely read his expression.  After a few heartbeats he started to get a little scared, until he saw the corner of the big cat's mouth twitch into the tiniest bit of a grin. He lifted the towel and resumed drying while he waited for Marty to answer.

When I had to be vindictive, mortals spoke of me in legends as the last thing someone sees before they're never seen again."  He turned back around, leaned down, and began drying one leg.  "Don't mind me!" he said, in an overly cheerful voice.  "Just dropping in real quick, nothing to worry about!"  He chuckled.  "Can you think what other reasons there might be?  You have spotted most of them already.  Sharpen your focus: why are you _still_ sober?"

Marty thought about this for a moment, looking down at the flagstones in front of him.  "There is...more left to do?"

The tiger nodded.  "Remember, I still have one more question for you," he reminded the sable as he switched to his other leg.

Marty was surprised.  "I thought you'd already asked it?"

The big cat waved this away.  "Small questions are incidental; I have not yet asked the big question."

"What's the big question?"

The tiger made a cautionary gesture.  "Do not rush into the big question, little one.  When a large wild creature asks a question like this, there must be due consideration."  He stopped, still leaned over his other leg, grinned, and fixed Marty with an unblinking predator's stare.

Marty gulped.  Those golden eyes were beautiful, penetrating.  They transfixed him.  He wanted to look away, but felt as if he were pinned in place where he bobbed in the hot spring.  

The big cat broke his stare with a wink, finished drying, and stood.  He raised his arms to each side, and with a flourish of his paw, a halo of fine mist erupted from his ebon fur and drifted gently to the ground.  He shook himself, and every hair in his lustrous coat fell into place, perfectly dry, as though freshly groomed.  "Ahh, much better."  He took a deep breath, and let it out smoothly.  "So, little one: the big question.  When you met me, you already had no control, and I _told_ you you were a pet.  You could not choose that for yourself, even if you would have; even though you did own it later.  Things had to happen just so to set several events in motion, and I needed to carry you through that.  This time, the difficult things we've talked through needed a setting of safety, and I call you 'pet' to invoke protection and guidance.  I haven't leaned on the imbalance of power implied."  

Marty nodded mutely.  He was following, but there was a sense of...not dread exactly, but of _great importance_ creeping up the back of his throat.  That what came next must be either wonderful, or terrible.  He quietly fought against a hope he dared not let form, lest he be crushed in its collapse.

"If you need that, reach for me, and I'll help you out of the spring; there is another towel here" - he gestured to the space behind the bushes - "that you can use.  We'll relax together as long as you need; I'll take you back to the hotel whenever you want."

The tiger continued.  "The other path is this.  You remember what I _have done_ with you.  But this time, fully sober, you are not a passenger.  The limits are only where we want them to be, and mine are _far_ broader than yours. I say this not because I _mean_ to hurt you, but because _it mustn't be a surprise that I can._  If I decide to hold you down and do _unspeakable things_ to you, there will be _no escape_ from my ministrations. Marty's breathing stopped.  There it was: hope was not only born, it was _triumphant._  Quickly, below the water line, he tugged on his belly fur to be sure he wasn't dreaming. Those golden eyes were watching him intently; he could barely look at them, instead keeping his gaze largely on the ground in front of the big cat, where he was headed.  One step at a time, he made his way slowly until he was standing before the tiger, dripping steadily onto the flagstones beneath him.  As he neared, he could hear the big cat purring quietly.

The big cat snapped his fingers, and pointed at the ground.  "Kneel."

Without looking up, Marty knelt before the tiger, knees together.  To his surprise, the tiger knelt with him, landing his knees just astride Marty's own.  The sable felt the big cat's paw grasp his chin gently, and lift his gaze to meet the tiger's own; Marty's breath quickened.  

"You are certain, little one?"
